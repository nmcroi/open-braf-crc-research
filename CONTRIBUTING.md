# Contributing

Start with an existing issue or propose a specific, falsifiable question. Read [scope and participation](docs/SCOPE-AND-PARTICIPATION.md) for the broader research areas and contributor routes. Contributions may be small: correct a DOI, identify an unavailable dataset, reproduce a denominator, or show why a hypothesis fails.

1. State your question and the issue you are addressing. Briefly claim the task to reduce duplication; no permission is needed to work in your own fork.
2. Record primary-source URLs, identifiers, retrieval dates and relevant versions or hashes. Distinguish full text, abstract, registry and secondary commentary.
3. Keep the patient, specimen, cell and treatment-arm denominators separate. Account for paired samples, missingness, selection, multiple testing and prior therapies. A difference in statistical significance is not a significant difference between groups.
4. Put code and aggregate outputs in a new analysis directory. Supply an exact run command, dependencies and expected output. Use portable paths; do not commit environments, credentials or downloaded clinical records.
5. For code, submit a pull request; for a source review without code, use the submission Issue template. Explain the finding, counterevidence and limitations. Explicitly identify AI assistance, copied author statistics, independently recomputed results and hypotheses. Negative findings and corrections are valuable.
6. Await independent review before describing a result as replicated. Maintainer acceptance is not peer review or clinical validation.

Never upload personal medical information or restricted datasets. Use primary-source links for papers unless redistribution rights are documented. Do not initiate patient contact, contact study teams, enroll patients, purchase services or run paid AI jobs on behalf of this project without explicit authorization.

Language: English for shared results. Clear, respectful language is expected. Treatment advice for individuals is outside scope.

Code contributions must be yours to share under MIT; original documentation under CC BY 4.0. Third-party material retains its own terms. By contributing, confirm you have the necessary rights.
